# Shift ZiJi from characters to talk and text

ZiJi already covers the part of Primary Chinese where an app has the most leverage: character recognition, character writing and word-level language use. It should keep that core and change its mastery rules. The larger risk for this child is elsewhere. In PSLE standard Chinese (华文), **oral and listening are 35% of the grade, open-ended reading and written interaction are 16%, and composition is 20%** ([SEAB 0005 syllabus](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/334/60adffb8-be52-41da-b09e-cdcc9130ffc7/0005_y26_sy.pdf)). In Singapore, oral Chinese tracks how much Mandarin a child speaks with adults, not how many literacy activities the child does ([Li & Tan, Cogent Education 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2331186X.2016.1161958)). So a weaker, English-home child who learns mostly alone on an iPad has to close the gap through daily Mandarin talk time and adult-supported reading, not through more character drills.

The best-supported strategies, in order of priority:

1. Spaced retrieval of characters and words, counting an item as known only after the child produces it (strong evidence).
2. Modelled repeated reading aloud (strong in general, moderate for Chinese).
3. Dialogic shared reading and explicit teaching of morphology and character structure (moderate; Chinese-specific trials).
4. Daily Mandarin conversation with a responsive adult (moderate).
5. Pinyin taught early and then faded, plus some handwriting (moderate).
6. Scaffolded talk-then-write composition (limited, but it is the best available).

For ZiJi, three things come first: a graded 朗读 read-aloud loop with high-quality recorded audio, short reading and listening passages built from characters the child already knows, and a parent loop that turns home English time into a few minutes of Mandarin talk a day. Two dates matter most. The end-P2 targets (about 750 characters recognised and 350 written) arrive in November 2026. The end-P4 subject-banding decision arrives in November 2028, and it decides whether the child stays on standard 华文 or is recommended for Foundation Chinese.

## PSLE 2030 grades characters least and conversation most

The 2024 syllabus keeps two stages: a foundation stage (P1–4) and an orientation stage (P5–6). It sets targets for six skills (listening, speaking, oral interaction, reading, writing and written interaction) plus values and culture (人文素养) and general competencies (通用能力). The P1–4 focus for 华文 is basic reading and sentence- and paragraph-level writing, "着重聆听、说话、口语互动、识字和写字的训练" (with emphasis on listening, speaking, oral interaction, character recognition and handwriting) ([MOE 2024 syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/primary/cl-syllabus-pri-2024.pdf)).

The 2024 syllabus **raised the lower-primary recognition targets to 700–750 characters (from 600–650) and 1300–1350 (from 1200–1300)**. It left the six writing bands (300–350 up to 1400–1500) unchanged from 2015 ([MOE 2024 syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/primary/cl-syllabus-pri-2024.pdf); [MOE 2015 syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/primary/chinese-primary-2015.pdf)). The text extraction lost the year columns of these tables, so the grade for each band has to be inferred. The 欢乐伙伴2.0 character lists that ZiJi already uses give an inference with high confidence for P2 and P4:

| Level | 华文: recognise | 华文: write | 高级华文: write |
|---|---|---|---|
| End-P2 (Nov 2026) | ~750 (the lists reach 746) | ~350 (the lists reach 350) | ~435 |
| End-P4 (Nov 2028) | 1300–1350 | 700–750 | 950–1000 |
| End-P6 (PSLE 2030) | 1600–1700 | 1000–1100 | 1400–1500 |

The skill targets beyond characters are the bigger gap. Listening runs from telling apart tones and syllables, to understanding who, when, where and why, to inferring and predicting. Speaking runs from clear pronunciation, to organised retelling and topic talk, to suitable volume, pace and intonation. Oral interaction covers greeting, requesting, apologising, persuading, discussing and turn-taking (话轮转换). Reading includes using pinyin and pictures, reading aloud fluently and with expression, finding main ideas and implied meaning, and evaluating. Writing explicitly includes "能使用汉语拼音进行输入（如汉字、词语、句子等）" (typing characters, words and sentences with pinyin input); 2024 added 词语 (words) to the 2015 wording. Written interaction covers cards, notes, messages, email and online posts. Values and culture (人文素养) list values (family, respect, perseverance, responsibility), Chinese festivals and stories, Singapore local culture and cross-cultural awareness, with no grade split ([MOE 2024 syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/primary/cl-syllabus-pri-2024.pdf)). MOE described the 2024 curriculum as using more animation, games and real-life tasks, and said it was working with industry on an AI tool for oracy practice outside school ([MOE press release, Aug 2023](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20230826-primary-mother-tongue-languages-curriculum-to-nurture-21st-century-competencies-in-students); [The Star/ST](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2023/08/27/new-curriculum-for-mother-tongue-languages-for-primary-1-pupils-in-singapore-from-2024)).

School assessment is light until P3. **P1 and P2 have had no weighted assessments or exams since 2019** ([MOE, Sep 2018](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20180928-learn-for-life-preparing-our-students-to-excel-beyond-exam-results)). Mid-year exams were removed at all levels by 2023 ([MOE, Mar 2022](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/forum-letter-replies/20220317-removal-of-mid-year-exams-will-help-nurture-joy-for-learning)). This child therefore meets weighted assessments and an end-of-year exam for the first time in P3 (2027). School components are typically 听写 (dictation), oral (朗读 plus 看图说话 and/or conversation), language use, comprehension, and 看图写话 or composition. One P2 oral described in a school snippet used short reading-aloud sentences, picture description and conversation ([ACS(J) P2 handbook](https://www.acsj.moe.edu.sg/files/Primary_2__2026_Assessment_Handbook.pdf)), though formats vary by school. The parent's own school booklet is the authority.

The PSLE baseline is the format SEAB still lists for 2026, marked "For Examination from 2017". **No change has been announced for the 2024-syllabus cohorts** ([SEAB formats 2026](https://www.seab.gov.sg/psle/psle-formats-examined-in-2026/)).

| PSLE 华文 component | Marks / weight | What is actually tested |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 写作 | 40 / 20% | 命题作文 (topic) or 看图作文 (picture) composition, ≥100 characters, 50 min. **An SEAB-approved print or electronic dictionary is allowed** |
| Paper 2 语文应用 + 短文填空 | 40 / 20% | MCQ on pinyin, characters, words and sentences, plus a cloze |
| Paper 2 阅读理解一 + 完成对话 | 18 / 9% | MCQ comprehension; completing a dialogue |
| Paper 2 阅读理解二 | 32 / 16% | Open-ended questions on a functional text and a passage, including 书面互动 (written interaction) |
| Paper 3 口试 | 50 / 25% | 朗读 (reading aloud) 20 + **会话 (conversation) based on a video clip** 30, after 10 min of preparation |
| Paper 3 听力 | 20 / 10% | 10 MCQ on dialogues, notices, ads, instructions and stories |

Source: [SEAB 0005](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/334/60adffb8-be52-41da-b09e-cdcc9130ffc7/0005_y26_sy.pdf).

The table has three implications. First, **看图说话 (picture talk) does not appear at PSLE**. It is a school-level step in P1–P4 toward the PSLE video conversation and toward 看图作文. Second, the dictionary allowance makes pinyin input a practical exam skill in Paper 1, not just a syllabus line. Third, Higher Chinese (高级华文) has no oral or listening paper and expects a narrative of more than 200 characters ([SEAB 0015](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/334/fab96888-7d2b-4203-8424-d82aad41d039/0015_y26_sy.pdf)). Foundation Chinese is 55% oral ([SEAB 0025](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/334/3b39adaa-5c92-4032-9796-8fefe30ecbae/0025_y26_sy.pdf)). End-P4 subject-based banding recommends the P5 level from P4 results ([MOE SBB](https://www.moe.gov.sg/primary/curriculum/subject-based-banding)). For a child below class level, the practical target is to secure standard 华文 at P4. Higher Chinese is a later option, not the plan. ZiJi should default this child to the 华文 track and its 350-character writing target.

One uncertainty affects all long-range planning. The 2024 syllabus pegs the PSLE to the core-module level, and SEAB will probably confirm or revise the format around 2027–2028, ahead of the first cohort's 2029 PSLE. If it changes, the likeliest areas are the ones the 2024 syllabus strengthened: digital literacy (pinyin input at word level, online posts) and authentic video and functional texts. That is an inference, not an announcement.

## Research backs retrieval, morphology, modelled reading and talk time

### General learning science supports ZiJi's core, with caveats on how mastery is judged

Retrieval practice and spacing are among the most robust findings in education. In 222 classroom studies, quizzing raised achievement by **g = 0.50 overall but g = 0.33 in elementary school** ([Yang et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349906849_Testing_quizzing_boosts_classroom_learning_A_systematic_and_meta-analytic_review)). Spacing benefits verbal learning, more so at longer delays ([L2 spacing meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358406370_The_Effects_of_Spaced_Practice_on_Second_Language_Learning_A_Meta-Analysis)). For young children, an **expanding schedule beat equal spacing at a 24-hour delay** ([Vlach et al. 2014](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3995866)). ZiJi's SRS is therefore well founded. The caveat is that no child-specific evidence exists on SRS parameters, and none on Chinese characters for children. The intervals are adult-derived guesses.

Two findings should change how ZiJi decides that a child "knows" something. First, app effects measured on the app's own tasks run about **0.42 SD larger than effects on standardised tests**, and constrained skills such as letter or character knowledge gain about 0.17 SD more than unconstrained skills such as vocabulary and comprehension ([Kim et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23328584211004183)). A 141-study update found a pooled d = 0.40, with smaller effects in randomised trials and larger samples, and found that **dosage did not predict effect size** ([Gilbert, Young & Kim 2026](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1579)). Second, errorful guessing helps memory for items but not for associations ([J. Memory & Language 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749596X18300858)), and character–sound–meaning links are associations. Together these say that four-option multiple-choice accuracy is weak evidence, that in-session accuracy is not learning, and that ZiJi's own dashboards will flatter the child. The fix is to count an item as mastered only after the child recalls or produces it (reads it aloud, writes it, types its pinyin, or picks it from many distractors) on separate days, and to validate against school 听写 and unseen text.

Feedback works: **d = 0.48 overall, and d = 0.99 when the feedback is high in information** ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456/)). ZiJi's explanations after mistakes, with pinyin and English, are the right instinct. They work best when they show why: the distinguishing component, the radical's meaning, a replay of the tone. Interleaving helps tell apart similar categories, but children benefit less from it than adults ([PMC12470837](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12470837/)). That supports ZiJi's look-alike drill (字辨) as a mixed review after characters are first introduced in blocks.

Motivation design carries real evidence. Expected rewards that are contingent on engagement **undermine intrinsic motivation in children (d = −0.46), more than in adults** ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)). Gamification overall has moderate positive effects ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://d-nb.info/1202307655/34)). In digital storybooks, **interactive features reduced learning whether or not they were relevant to the story** ([Takacs et al. 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276334037_Benefits_and_Pitfalls_of_Multimedia_and_Interactive_Features_in_Technology-Enhanced_Storybooks_A_Meta-Analysis)), while plain narrated picture stories beat traditional materials for children reading without an adult ([Takacs et al. 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4253734/)). ZiJi's stars, streaks and Truffle are acceptable if they report competence ("you can now read 412 characters") rather than reward time spent, if streaks forgive rest days, and if the mascot stays out of the learning moment. No evidence exists on streaks or mascots for 7–8-year-olds, so these are reasoned guardrails rather than findings.

### Chinese-literacy research favours morphology, structure, some writing and faded pinyin

The most Chinese-specific finding is that **morphological awareness predicts character reading and comprehension more strongly in Chinese than in English**. Phonological awareness shows the opposite pattern ([PMC6367908](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6367908/); [Sci. Stud. Reading meta-analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2024.2419541)). Morphological awareness here covers compounding (组词), homophones and homographs. For L2 learners, phonological, orthographic and morphological awareness all correlate moderately with word reading (r ≈ .36–.41). **Phonological awareness matters most for beginners (r = .49 with under 2 years of learning, against .29 later)** ([Chen & Zhao 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8966685/)). That implies a shift over primary school: sound and tone in P1–P2, then structure and meaning. Interventions confirm the causal direction for L1 children. Explicit morphology instruction for 169 Beijing children in Grades 1–2 "substantially improved" literacy ([Wu, Anderson et al.](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/morphological-awareness-and-chinese-childrens-literacy-developmen/)). Explicit teaching of character structure and word morphology made first graders better at **writing characters from memory** ([Packard et al. 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-006-9003-4)). In a randomised trial in Hong Kong, dialogic reading built vocabulary, and **adding morphology training to it raised character recognition** ([Chow et al. 2008](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Dialogic-reading-and-morphology-training-in-Chinese-Chow-McBride-Chang/ffd598c97adea8a40a6cab6d17ccaa34c9e7da98)).

Handwriting is not optional decoration. In L1 children, copying and writing skill relates to reading ([Tan et al. 2005, via PNAS listing](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1213586110)). Training studies show that **writing strengthens orthographic representations while alphabetic coding (pinyin) strengthens phonology** ([Guan, Perfetti et al. 2011](https://www.academia.edu/18046754/Writing_strengthens_orthography_and_alphabetic_coding_strengthens_phonology_in_learning_to_read_Chinese)). A 2013 PNAS paper linked children's heavy use of pinyin input to their reading development; the notes recall the association as negative but could not re-verify its direction ([Tan et al. 2013](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1213586110)). Pinyin typing should therefore be added on top of handwriting, never instead of it. Stroke order has no separate causal evidence; its value is as consistent motor encoding of components. Nothing compares stylus tracing with paper writing for children, so ZiJi's trace → hint → recall sequence is plausible but unproven. Some paper writing for 听写 stays worthwhile.

On pinyin, Grade 1 pinyin teaching coincided with phoneme awareness rising from chance to 70%, and tone awareness reached 74% ([Shu, Peng & McBride-Chang 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2007.00654.x)). For L2 learners, **showing the character about 5 seconds before its pinyin and English beat showing them together** ([Chung 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1025560327860)). On-screen pinyin also shows an expertise reversal: it helps novices and becomes redundant as learners advance ([Computers in Human Behavior 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563210001251)). The claim that pinyin is a "crutch" rests on opinion. The evidence points to fading it: show it for new characters, hide it for known ones, and reveal it on tap or after a delay.

For fluency, repeated reading gives **fluency ES 0.64 and comprehension 0.42**. It works better with an adult, with a model reading first, and with a cue to read for meaning ([Therrien 2004](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249835323_Fluency_and_Comprehension_Gains_as_a_Result_of_Repeated_Reading_A_Meta-Analysis)). In Chinese immersion classes, repeated paired reading beat comprehension activities on fluency, character recognition and comprehension, and the gains transferred to new texts ([Knell & West](https://benjamins.com/catalog/jicb.00009.kne)). Repeated reading with explicit prosody modelling produced **large gains in character reading** ([Wang 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9817.70017)). This is the strongest case for ZiJi's parked 朗读 feature, and the strongest case against the weak iPad TTS voice. The active ingredient is a good prosodic model.

Oral language is where an app reaches its limit. Among 376 Singaporean Chinese 6-year-olds, **more Mandarin with parents predicted oral proficiency, while more Chinese literacy activities predicted written proficiency** ([Li & Tan 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2331186X.2016.1161958)). Responsive adult turn-taking is a core predictor of language growth ([Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015](https://kathyhirshpasek.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2015/09/Psychological-Science-in-the-Public-Interest-2015-Hirsh-Pasek-3-34.pdf)). This child is not unusual. English was the home language for 52% of Chinese Singaporeans aged 5–14, per the census cited by Li & Tan ([Cogent Education 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2331186X.2016.1161958)), and the 2010 MTL review responded by shifting the curriculum toward oral interaction ([NIE repository](https://repository.nie.edu.sg/bitstreams/0d997a2b-de4d-42f8-9a39-39f890921f51/download)). Speech recognition can listen to children read; Project LISTEN produced fluency gains in a small study ([LISTEN study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228364770_Tutoring_Bilingual_Students_with_an_Automated_Reading_Tutor_That_Listens)). But child speech remains hard for ASR ([arXiv 2503.04710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04710)), and no evidence was found on ASR judging Mandarin tones in children.

Composition is the weakest evidence base. Singapore has a controlled trial of strategies-based writing instruction, but its effect sizes were not retrievable ([SBI study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306059809_Taking_Stock_of_the_Effects_of_Strategies-Based_Instruction_on_Writing_in_Chinese_and_English_in_Singapore_Primary_Classrooms)), plus SCCL toolkits for P3 paragraph writing and P5 descriptive writing ([SCCL](https://www.sccl.sg/en/publication-and-jcle/teaching-toolkit/primary-school-teaching-toolkit)). No controlled study of 造句 practice or of imitating model texts in Chinese primary composition was found. Sentence frames, oral rehearsal before writing, and guided paragraph structure are the best available approach, with grade C/D evidence.

## Tuition centres sell structure and talk time, and templates hit a ceiling

The large chains (Berries, Confucius, Wang Learning Centre, Tien Hsia, Hua and others) converge on one product: a **weekly 2–3 hour class of 6–8 pupils**, worksheets keyed to 欢乐伙伴, weekly 听写, foundation work in P1–P3, and mock papers, model essays and recorded oral mocks in P4–P6 ([SunnyCityKids survey](https://www.sunnycitykids.com/blog/chinese-tuition-centres-in-singapore); [Hua Language Centre](https://hua.com.sg/programmes/primary/)). The visible differences are branding: play and drama (Berries, Chengzhu's EduDrama) against results and drilling (Wang's "mandatory weekly composition and precision-marked oral drilling") ([Chengzhu](https://www.chengzhu.edu.sg/mandarin-programmes/school-years-programmes/creative-writing/); [tuitionreview.com](https://tuitionreview.com/2025/07/14/wang-learning-centre-wang-laoshi-review-does-it-really-improve-psle-chinese-scores/)). **Every outcome claim found (">70% distinctions", "78% AL1–AL2", "15–25% in 8 weeks") lacks a denominator, a baseline or a method.** No independent evaluation of any centre exists in the sources found.

Judged against the research, several common practices are evidence-aligned. Themed vocabulary clusters reviewed "today, tomorrow, next week, next month" ([Ancourage](https://ancourage.academy/articles/primary-chinese-tips-singapore)) are spaced retrieval. Radical-based character teaching, such as Le Xue's "3R", is morphology and structure. Short passages written from textbook vocabulary (Hua) are graded reading. Recorded oral mocks sent to parents and individual composition feedback that tracks recurring errors (Ancourage) are informative feedback with a parent in the loop. Picture discussion and literature discussion before writing (Chengzhu) are oral-to-written scaffolding. The genuinely useful thing tuition offers an English-home child is **paid Mandarin interaction minutes with a responsive adult**. Bilingual explanation for English-home pupils (Ancourage, among others) helps with access.

Other practices are exam drilling with a known ceiling. Examples are memorised openers and closers, forced insertion of 好词好句 (stock "good words and phrases"), memorised model essays, scripted video-conversation answers on predicted topics, and past-paper volume without strategy teaching. The industry admits this. One centre that teaches 起承转合 (a four-part narrative structure) warns that "a student who can use 20 phrases naturally and correctly will outscore one who has memorised 100 phrases but forces them into inappropriate contexts" ([Ancourage](https://ancourage.academy/articles/psle-chinese-composition-score-al1-strategy-singapore)). A vendor of oral practice reports that examiners probe with "你同意吗？" (do you agree?) to break memorised scripts ([PSLEPrep](https://psleprep.sg/blog)); this is vendor commentary, not SEAB's. Chinese-language media quote a Singapore teacher who is "很无奈" (at a loss) over children who drill papers, memorise model essays and still write awkward sentences ([163.com](https://m.163.com/dy/article/H0UJAK6H0524A4QN.html)). Structural frames sit in the middle. 起承转合, or opinion plus reason plus personal experience, are reasonable planning scaffolds for a weak writer when the child generates the content. They become harmful when they replace vocabulary depth and reading volume.

The most telling gap is what no centre offers: **a sustained extensive-reading programme with volume targets**. Comprehension teaching appears to be mostly practice papers plus correction, and no centre publishes a framework for answering comprehension questions. For a weaker English-home child, that leaves the most important unconstrained skill (reading volume that feeds vocabulary, comprehension and composition) unserved by both the app market and the tuition market. ZiJi can fill that gap cheaply.

## Thirteen strategies, ranked and mapped to every outcome

The ranking weighs three things together: evidence strength, the outcome's PSLE weight, and how far this child is from the outcome. Evidence labels: **Strong** means several meta-analyses that include children; **Moderate** means Chinese-specific controlled trials or child meta-analyses with caveats; **Limited** means correlational, adult-only, or practitioner evidence.

| # | Strategy | Outcomes served | Evidence | Who leads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily Mandarin conversation with a responsive adult (5–15 min; topic of the day; 我觉得…因为… frames) | Speaking, oral interaction, listening, video 会话 (25% + 10%) | Moderate (SG correlational; interaction research) | Parent / tutor |
| 2 | Modelled repeated reading aloud of short graded texts: listen → read along → read alone → re-read, plus one meaning question | 朗读, fluency, recognition, comprehension | Strong in general; Moderate for Chinese | App, with a parent listening |
| 3 | Spaced retrieval of characters and words, with mastery counted only on production across days | Recognition and writing targets, 听写, 语文应用 | Strong (general); Limited for hanzi in children | App |
| 4 | Explicit morphology and structure: 组词 families, homophone sorting, radical meaning cues, phonetic components | Recognition, writing from memory, vocabulary, 语文应用 | Moderate (L1 RCTs; L2 correlational) | App |
| 5 | Dialogic shared reading: an adult asks open questions about a picture book in Mandarin | Vocabulary, comprehension, oral expression | Moderate (Chinese RCT) | Parent |
| 6 | Some handwriting of high-frequency characters, focused on components | 写字 target, recognition, 听写 | Moderate (L1 correlational and training) | App (tracing) plus paper |
| 7 | Pinyin taught to automaticity in P1–P2, then faded (new items only, tap or delay to reveal) | Listening (tones), 朗读, pinyin MCQ, pinyin input | Moderate (L1 longitudinal; adult L2 experiments) | App |
| 8 | Wide reading of graded texts made from known characters, with comprehension checks moving from literal to inferential | 阅读理解 (25% of PSLE), vocabulary, culture | Moderate (multimedia stories); Limited (Chinese thresholds) | App, plus library books with a parent |
| 9 | Informative feedback on every error (component, tone, meaning), with a retry | All practice skills | Strong | App |
| 10 | Talk-then-write composition: 看图说话 aloud → 写话 sentences → paragraph frame → 起承转合 at P5–6, with human feedback | 造句, 看图作文, 命题作文 (20%) | Limited (SG toolkits; SBI) | App scaffolds; tutor or teacher marks |
| 11 | Short listening practice on PSLE text types (dialogues, notices, ads, stories) | 听力 (10%), 完成对话 | Limited (general comprehension logic) | App |
| 12 | Competence-based motivation: forgiving streaks, mastery reports, autonomy over review | Persistence, learning competencies | Strong (reward undermining); Limited (streaks) | App |
| 13 | Exam-format familiarisation late (P5–6): timed papers, video 会话 mocks, dictionary use | All PSLE components | Limited (practical) | Tuition / school |

Values and culture deserve no separate drill. They are best delivered through content: festival stories, 成语 origin stories, Singapore pioneer and multiracial stories, and family values as the subjects of reading passages, listening clips and conversation prompts. One activity then serves two outcomes, and the content matches the recurring PSLE conversation themes (family, school, environment, technology) that centres prepare ([Ancourage](https://ancourage.academy/articles/primary-chinese-tips-singapore)). Video-based conversation is the outcome with the least direct evidence on how to teach it. The defensible approach combines strategy 1 (real conversation minutes) with an opinion–reason–experience frame introduced at P4, and practises the follow-up "你同意吗？" probe instead of memorising scripts.

## Apps own the constrained skills; humans must own conversation

The research draws a clean line. **Apps reliably move constrained skills** (characters, pinyin, word forms) and move unconstrained skills (vocabulary in use, comprehension, oral expression) much less ([Kim et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23328584211004183)). Oral proficiency follows conversation with adults, not literacy activity ([Li & Tan 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2331186X.2016.1161958)). Adults amplify repeated reading ([Therrien 2004](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249835323_Fluency_and_Comprehension_Gains_as_a_Result_of_Repeated_Reading_A_Meta-Analysis)). Apps support learning when they are "socially interactive" within a supported goal ([Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015](https://kathyhirshpasek.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2015/09/Psychological-Science-in-the-Public-Interest-2015-Hirsh-Pasek-3-34.pdf)).

| Outcome | ZiJi should | Parent / school / tuition should |
|---|---|---|
| 识字 / 写字 targets | Run SRS in textbook order with a production-based mastery rule; teach morphology and structure; trace → recall; sync with school 听写 through the importer | Hold weekly paper 听写; praise specific progress |
| 聆听 | Short PSLE-type clips with recorded audio; tone discrimination; dialogue completion | Mandarin cartoons and audiobooks during downtime |
| 朗读 | Model → read along → record → play back → re-read; graded passages; 多音字 and tone-sandhi cues | Listen to one recording a day and respond to its meaning, not to errors |
| 看图说话 / 会话 / video 会话 | Picture prompts with question ladders and connective cards; record answers; from P4, video clips with opinion prompts and a "你同意吗？" follow-up | **Daily conversation minutes**; a Mandarin-speaking tutor or relative; school oral practice |
| 阅读理解 | Graded passages from known characters, literal to inferential questions, open-ended answers typed or spoken | Dialogic reading of picture books; library Chinese books |
| 造句 / 看图作文 | Word → frame → sentence builder; picture → oral telling → sentences → paragraph; model sentences from passages already read | Teacher or tutor marking with error tracking; from P4–P5, weekly composition with feedback |
| 书面互动 / pinyin input | Pinyin typing of words and sentences; cards, notes and messages from P3–P4; electronic-dictionary lookup practice | Real messages to grandparents or relatives in Chinese |
| 人文素养 | Culture and values as passage and conversation content | Festivals and outings, talked about in Mandarin |

The split carries one uncomfortable implication. If the home cannot supply Mandarin conversation, the parent should buy it, through a conversational tutor, a Mandarin-speaking caregiver or relative, or an oral-heavy small-group class. At P2–P3 that matters more than exam-oriented tuition. Composition-feedback tuition becomes worth its cost from about P4, when 写段 (paragraph writing) and the banding decision converge. This is a reasoned inference from the evidence, not a tested finding.

## A four-phase road from P2 to PSLE 2030

| Phase | Dates | Priority outcomes | App focus | Human focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Close the P2 gap | Oct 2026 – Nov 2026 | ~750 recognised, ~350 written; pinyin and tones automatic | SRS on overdue and weak items; enable 朗读 on textbook lessons; pinyin typing of single words | 10 min of conversation daily; listen to one read-aloud a day | End-P2 targets met on production, checked on the school's 听写 and on an unseen P2 text |
| 2. Read for meaning | P3, 2027 | Recognition toward 1031 (lists), writing toward 569; 看图说话 → 写话; first weighted assessments | Graded passages with comprehension; listening clips; 造句 builder; culture passages; pinyin fades on known characters | Dialogic reading 2–3 times a week; school oral practice; consider an oral-heavy class | P3 end-of-year exam components; reads an unseen 100-character passage aloud with few errors |
| 3. Secure 华文 at banding | P4, 2028 | 1300–1350 recognised, 700–750 written; paragraph writing; open-ended comprehension; video 会话 introduced | Opinion–reason–experience speaking prompts with video; 书面互动 tasks; inferential questions; 完成对话 | Composition feedback (tuition or teacher); recorded oral mocks reviewed with a parent | **End-P4 SBB (Nov 2028)**: avoid a Foundation recommendation |
| 4. PSLE readiness | P5–P6, 2029–2030 | 1600–1700 recognised, 1000–1100 written; ≥100-character composition; full PSLE components | Timed component practice; dictionary-lookup drills; 起承转合 planner; listening sets; spaced review of all prior characters | Weekly marked compositions; video 会话 mocks with real follow-up questions; past papers from mid-P6 | PSLE 2030 (confirm the format with SEAB around 2027–28) |

The sequencing follows the shift in phonological awareness: sound and tone first, structure and meaning next ([Chen & Zhao 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8966685/)). It also follows the school's own progression from 写话 (P1–2) to 写段 (P3–4) to full composition ([MOE 2024 syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/primary/cl-syllabus-pri-2024.pdf)). Exam drilling is held back to phase 4 on purpose. Before then the binding constraints are input volume and talk time, not familiarity with the format.

## What ZiJi should build next

The priorities below rank each feature by four things: its share of the PSLE grade, how large the child's gap is, the strength of the evidence, and how feasible it is for a self-paced iPad app.

| P0 | **朗读 read-aloud loop** (un-park it) | 朗读, fluency, recognition | Strong / Moderate | Recorded native or high-quality audio, never the weak TTS, for passages. | P0 | **Audio quality upgrade** | Listening, 朗读, tones | Moderate (prosody modelling) | Pre-recorded open-source clips are fine for characters and words if checked for 多音字 and tone accuracy. Passages need natural sentence prosody, including 一/不 and third-tone sandhi |

| P2 | **看图说话 → 写话 builder** (un-park picture-talk) | Speaking, 造句, 看图作文 | Limited | Picture plus question ladder (谁, 什么时候, 在哪里, 做什么, 结果怎样, 心情). Child answers aloud and records, then builds sentences from tiles or pinyin input. Connective cards (因为…所以, 先…然后…最后) |

The fact sheet also needs three small corrections to existing features. First, keep the 华文 track as the default for this child; the 高级华文 writing list (435 by end-P2) would overload a weaker learner. Second, make the worksheet importer the bridge to school. It should pull each week's 听写 list into the SRS queue so that app practice and the school's dictation reinforce each other, which is the main way an app's narrow gains reach school outcomes. Third, the pinyin-and-English explanations after mistakes should show the character first and the pinyin a beat later, to follow the character-first timing that helped L2 learners ([Chung 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1025560327860)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes this project. ZiJi's strongest features are aimed at the slice of Chinese where apps reliably work and where PSLE puts the least direct weight, while this child's biggest deficit (spoken Mandarin from an English-speaking home) sits in the slice where apps work least. That makes ZiJi's most valuable next step to become a hub that organises human practice: it can model reading aloud, record the child, hand the parent a few conversation prompts, and turn known characters into stories. The real test of the app is whether the child reads an unseen passage aloud and answers "为什么？" (why?) in Mandarin, not how many cards the child has cleared.

Three unknowns should shape the roadmap. SEAB has not confirmed the PSLE 2030 format, and any change will probably favour digital and authentic tasks, which argues for building pinyin input and video conversation in a format-agnostic way. No study has tested SRS parameters, stylus tracing or ASR tone feedback with young learners of Chinese, so ZiJi should log retention at a delay and compare it with school 听写 results. Over time it can become its own evidence. Finally, the tuition market offers structure and conversation minutes but no reading volume, so a family that pairs ZiJi's graded reading with a conversational tutor covers more of what the research supports than either tuition or the app does alone.
